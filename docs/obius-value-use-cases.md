# Obius: Value Proposition & Industry Use Cases

## What Makes Obius Valuable

### 1. Section-First Architecture

Traditional note apps treat notes as monolithic documents — long walls of text that become unwieldy over time. Obius breaks content into **atomic sections**: discrete, reorderable, nestable units of thought. Each section can be collapsed, color-coded by intent (idea, task, question, reference, important), and reorganized with drag-and-drop.

**Why it matters:** Users spend less time scrolling and reformatting, and more time thinking. Complex ideas stay structured from the moment they're captured.

### 2. Local-First, Offline-Ready

Obius stores all data on-device before syncing to the cloud. There is no dependency on an internet connection to read, write, or organize notes.

**Why it matters:** Users in low-connectivity environments — field researchers, travelers, workshop facilitators — never lose access to their work. When connectivity returns, sync happens automatically with built-in conflict resolution.

### 3. Privacy by Design

- Notes live on the device first.
- Cloud sync uses row-level security so users can only access their own data.
- An optional **privacy mode** keeps all data local-only with no cloud sync at all.
- No data is sold or shared.

**Why it matters:** Professionals handling sensitive information — legal, medical, financial — can trust that their notes stay private without relying on third-party promises.

### 4. AI That Assists, Not Controls

AI features in Obius are strictly opt-in:

- **Semantic search** finds sections by meaning, not just keyword matching (powered by vector embeddings).
- **Auto-generated titles and summaries** reduce busywork when organizing large note collections.
- **Related section discovery** surfaces connections across notes the user may not have noticed.

AI never runs in the background without user initiation.

**Why it matters:** Users get the benefits of intelligent search and organization without sacrificing control or triggering unwanted processing of their private content.

### 5. Multi-Device Sync with Conflict Resolution

An append-only changelog and per-device sync cursors ensure that edits from multiple devices merge cleanly. The split-storage engine separates hot and cold data for efficient synchronization.

**Why it matters:** Users who work across an iPhone, iPad, and desktop environment get a seamless experience without data loss or manual merge headaches.

### 6. Structured Intent Through Color-Coding

Sections carry an **intent** attribute — idea, important, question, task, reference, or default. This adds a lightweight metadata layer without forcing users into rigid templates.

**Why it matters:** When reviewing notes days or weeks later, intent markers immediately communicate what each piece of content represents, reducing cognitive load.

## Industry Use Cases

### Legal

| Scenario | How Obius Helps |
|---|---|
| **Case research and preparation** | Attorneys organize case notes into sections by witness, exhibit, or legal argument. Collapse completed threads; expand active ones. |
| **Client meeting notes** | Capture discussion points as discrete sections. Tag questions for follow-up, mark important commitments, and reference relevant statutes — all with intent color-coding. |
| **Privileged and confidential material** | Privacy mode ensures sensitive case notes never leave the device. No cloud sync means no risk of inadvertent disclosure. |
| **Cross-referencing precedents** | Semantic search finds related sections across hundreds of case notes by meaning, surfacing relevant precedents the attorney may have forgotten. |

### Healthcare

| Scenario | How Obius Helps |
|---|---|
| **Clinical observation notes** | Physicians and nurses capture observations as structured sections during rounds. Offline-first design works reliably in hospital dead zones. |
| **Research literature review** | Researchers organize papers and findings into nested, collapsible sections. Semantic search connects related findings across projects. |
| **Patient handoff documentation** | Section-based structure makes it easy to hand off organized, scannable notes between shifts. Intent markers highlight outstanding tasks and critical observations. |
| **Compliance and data privacy** | Local-first storage and privacy mode support HIPAA-conscious workflows where cloud transmission of patient-adjacent notes is a concern. |

### Education

| **Student research projects** | Students organize thesis research into sections by source, argument, and evidence. Semantic search surfaces connections across months of accumulated notes. |
| **Fieldwork documentation** | Students and researchers in the field — archaeology, ecology, geology — capture observations offline, with automatic sync when they return to connectivity. |
| **Classroom facilitation** | Workshop and seminar leaders capture participant contributions as live sections, reorganizing themes in real time during discussion. |

## Summary

Obius is built for people who think in structure, work across contexts, and need their tools to respect both their privacy and their workflow. Its combination of section-first architecture, local-first storage, optional AI assistance, and offline reliability makes it applicable wherever complex information needs to be captured, organized, and retrieved — from courtrooms to classrooms, clinics to construction sites.
